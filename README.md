1. What is the problem? 
Through a deeper analysis of the healthcare sector, it becomes clear that hospitals are facing a 
serious and recurring security problem: the theft and mismanagement of high-value and 
sensitive medical drugs. These include life-saving cancer treatments, narcotics used in surgeries, 
and other critical medications that are strictly controlled due to their medical and legal 
importance. 
In many public healthcare systems, especially in developing countries such as Egypt, hospital 
crises are no longer isolated or rare events. Instead, they have become systematic and repeated 
issues. These crises include medical errors, shortage of medical staff, lack of essential supplies, 
and most critically, the leakage and theft of medicines from hospital storage systems. 
For example, several reported cases in Egyptian public hospitals, including incidents at major 
institutions such as Assiut University Hospital, revealed significant discrepancies in drug 
inventories, with losses estimated in the millions of Egyptian pounds. These incidents were not 
only financial losses but also raised concerns about internal control systems, weak monitoring 
mechanisms, and the absence of real-time tracking for medical assets. Such cases highlight a 
critical gap in hospital infrastructure: the lack of a secure, intelligent system for controlling 
access to high-risk medications. 
This situation raises an essential question: Why do these problems continue to occur despite 
existing security procedures, and what technological gap allows such repeated losses? 
The answer to this question became the foundation for developing our proposed solution.
